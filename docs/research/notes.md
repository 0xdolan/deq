## Name and etymology

- The tattoo is called _deq_ (دەق) in Kurmanji, and also _xał_ (خاڵ) [wikipedia-deq]. SBS uses "deq/xal" [sbs-2022].
- The word is traced to Syriac _Daqqe_ / _Dyoqo_, "puncturing" [wikipedia-deq]. Kurdistan Chronicle gives Aramaic _Dyoqo_ [kurdistan-chronicle-2026].
- A tattooer is a _deqqak_ (masc.) / _deqqake_ (fem.), and a tattooed person is _medquk_ / _medquke_ [wikipedia-deq]. The Amargi uses "deqqars" for practitioners [amargi-2025]. The artist Elu signs as "DEKKAKE" [instagram-deq-ttt].
- Arabic: _vesm_ / _veshm_ [wikipedia-deq]; _wesm_ [indyturk-2020].
- Regional Turkish-language terms around Şanlıurfa include "dek", "daki", "vesm" and "döğün". This comes from Ahmet Bikiç's 2017 documentary, as listed in the deq-ttt archive [deq-ttt-archive]. Verify against the documentary itself.
- In Sorani, _xał_ also means a dot or full stop, and a maternal uncle [diplomatic-xal].

## Origins and antiquity

- The origin is unknown. Sources trace it to ancient Iraq (Mesopotamia) [wikipedia-deq], and say it predates Christianity and Islam [kurdistan-chronicle-2026].
- Aëtius of Amida (early 6th century, born in Amed/Diyarbakır) recorded a tattoo ink recipe: pine bark, corroded bronze and leek juice [wikipedia-deq]. Özgür Politika names the work _Medicae Artis Principes_ and gives the full recipe [ozgur-2021].
- Hammurabi's code, article 226, is linked to slave marking [wikipedia-deq; ozgur-2021]. Özgür Politika also links Babylonian slave marks and the star of Ishtar in the Eanna temple at Uruk [ozgur-2021].
- Diplomatic Magazine says experts date the practice to about 10,000 years ago, and that it was especially strong among nomadic Kurds and Ezidis [diplomatic-xal]. This figure is not sourced in the article; treat it as a claim, not a fact.
- Field records: Jacques de Morgan published engravings of Kurdish women's tattoos from Tchômaklou, Kilakân and Kialhours (figures 27–29, image set `de-morgan-*`). Henry Field drew hand, forearm and foot tattoos (`field-tattoo-drawings-*`). E. S. Drower recorded Ezidi tattoo designs (`drower-ezidi-tattoo-designs-01`). All three publications are marked **to verify** in sources.json.

## How it was made

- **Ink (most cited):** soot mixed with the breast milk of a mother who has a daughter [dailysabah-2016; kurdistan-chronicle-2026; aljazeera-2023; amargi-2025; indyturk-2020; ozgur-2021; natakallam-2024].
- **Other ingredients:**
  - liquid from a sheep's or goat's gallbladder [natgeo-2015; dailysabah-2016];
  - ox bile or herbs [kurdistan-chronicle-2026];
  - herbs, lamp soot and animal intestines [wikipedia-deq; aljazeera-2023; natakallam-2024];
  - in Kobanî and Shingal, tree stems and milk [diplomatic-xal].
- **Tools and healing:** a sewing needle (_derzî_). The wound scabs, and the mark turns greenish [indyturk-2020].
- **Age:** girls were tattooed between about 8 and 12 [natgeo-2015].
- **Who tattooed:** travelling, nomadic or "gypsy" women [natgeo-2015]; Dom women [aljazeera-2023]; _Qeraçî_ [indyturk-2020]; Romani deqqars [amargi-2025]. Use each community name as the source gives it, and do not generalise.

**Motif names in Kurdish, as given by sources:**

- NaTakallam (Kurmanji) [natakallam-2024]: _roj_ (sun), _heyv_ (moon), _stêrin_ (stars), _xezal_ (gazelle), _marin_ (snakes), _rewik_ (plants), _meqes_ / _cawbirr_ (scissors), _bûkoke_ (dolls).
- Wikipedia lists these Yezidi patterns, after Drower [wikipedia-deq]: comb, cross, gazelle, _daqqayeh_, sandgrouse foot, moon, doll, spindle, _res daqq_, _dimlich_.
- Diplomatic Magazine lists sun, moon, star, gazelle, snake and peacock [diplomatic-xal].
- Özgür Politika lists the shapes women wear: birds, gazelle, dot, triangle, octagon, square, a divided rectangle and dotted geometric shapes. Men wear scorpion, snake, pistol and sword [ozgur-2021].

## Placement

- **Women:** chin, hands, nostrils, forehead, neck, ankles and breasts [dailysabah-2016]. In Karacadağ and Çınar: forehead, chin, ankle, hand and upper chest [ozgur-2021].
- **Men:** arms and hands [dailysabah-2016]; temples [ozgur-2021]; hands, arms and temples [diplomatic-xal].

## Regions and communities

- **Main area:** Mardin, Diyarbakır (Amed) and Şanlıurfa [wikipedia-deq; dailysabah-2016]. The practice reached Syria from Urfa through Kobanî [natgeo-2015].
- **Towns named:** Siverek, Viranşehir, Hilvan, Derik, Mazıdağı, Kızıltepe, Ömerli and Nusaybin [ozgur-2021].
- **Dersim:** Elu (Zaza Alevi from Dersim) and the documentary with Çiler Kılıç [natakallam-2024; deq-ttt-documentary].
- **Ezidi traditions:** sun, moon, stars and peacock [hilton-tattooed-women; dailysabah-2016]; the Drower plate (to verify).
- **Field work:** de Morgan worked in Tchômaklou, Kilakân and Kialhours. Henry Field worked in Kurdistan and Kermanshah in the 1950s (from the summary of earlier reading; verify against field-1958).

## Decline

- The practice largely stopped about 50 years ago, by the mid-1960s [natgeo-2015; hilton-tattooed-women].
- Reasons given:
  - religious views that deq is _haram_, and the sense that it is old-fashioned; henna replaced it [wikipedia-deq];
  - assimilation [natakallam-2024];
  - stigma, migration and urbanisation [kurdistan-chronicle-2026].
- Deq was forced on Armenian women, according to the artist Arjin [amargi-2025]. Handle this with care, and cite it as her account.

## Revival

- **Fatê (Fatma) Temel:** did her first deq in 2018 in Derik [aljazeera-2023], and has run a studio in Sur, Diyarbakır, since November 2021. She charged about 200 Turkish lira per deq and saw two or three customers a day [aljazeera-2023].
- **Elu (deq-ttt, Berlin):** hand-pokes deq and sells temporary deq sheets and gift vouchers [deq-ttt-home; natakallam-2024; instagram-deq-ttt].
- **Deq Art Studio (Amed)** [instagram-deq-artstudio].
- **Sunya Deq studio (Istanbul):** run by the artist Arjin [amargi-2025].
- **Ahmet Yavuklu:** photographer and filmmaker who calls deq "a form of worship" [aljazeera-2023]. See his films [yt-yavuklu-kadim-izler; yt-yavuklu-part1; yt-yavuklu-last-marks; yt-yavuklu-channel].
- **Diaspora:** young Kurds in Australia use tattoos to show identity, according to Maria Tence [ausgeo-2010]. The article does not mention deq.
- **Video:** Al Jazeera Newsfeed [yt-aljazeera-newsfeed]; Great Big Story [yt-great-big-story].

## Disagreements to state in the story

1. **Ink recipe:** breast milk and soot is the common core. The additions differ: gallbladder liquid, ox bile, herbs, intestines, or tree stems.
2. **Etymology:** Wikipedia says Syriac; Kurdistan Chronicle says Aramaic. The Arabic form is spelled _vesm_ / _veshm_ / _wesm_.
3. **Quote wording:** Safi Haso's quote differs between sources ("that has" / "to have"). Quote the primary source (natgeo-2015).
4. **Names:** Fatê / Fatma Temel; the SBS image credit spells her surname "Metel" [sbs-2022]. Kobane / Kobani / Kobanî. Use _Kobanî_ in prose and keep each source's spelling in titles.
5. **Dating:** "about 10,000 years" [diplomatic-xal] is unsourced. Others say only "ancient" or "pre-Christian".
6. **Who tattooed:** Dom, Qeraçî, Romani and "gypsy" are used for overlapping groups. Do not merge them.
7. **Inverted V:** a tribal mark sized to the family group [natgeo-2015], or a motif whose meaning the speaker did not know [indyturk-2020].
